Aarti at home

A simple, traditional way to do the evening aarti, and which aarti to sing on which day.

What you need

  • A diya (lamp) with ghee or oil and a cotton wick, or a piece of camphor.
  • A puja thali: roli or kumkum, a few grains of rice (akshat), flowers, incense, a small bell.
  • A small pot of water (kalash or lota) and prasad: fruit, sweets or even sugar crystals.
  • Sit or stand facing the image or murti, ideally facing east or north. Nothing here is mandatory; a sincere heart is the one essential.

The usual order

  1. Light the lamp and recite Shubham Karoti.
  2. Remember Ganesha first: Vakratunda Mahakaya, then Jai Ganesh Deva.
  3. Sing the aarti of the day's deity or of the festival (see the guides below).
  4. Most families close with Om Jai Jagdish Hare.
  5. While the flame is still burning, chant Karpur Gauram.
  6. Offer the flame to everyone present: they pass their palms over it and touch their eyes and head.
  7. Finish with Kshama Prarthana and Sarve Bhavantu Sukhinah, then share the prasad.

Waving the lamp

  • Hold the thali in your right hand and move it in slow clockwise circles while you sing. Ring the bell with your left hand if you have one.
  • A widely taught custom is four circles at the deity's feet, two at the navel, one at the face, then seven around the whole form.
  • Keep the flame steady and the movement calm; the aarti is an offering of light, not a performance.

Reading the Roman text

ā ī ūLong vowels: ā as in 'father', ī as in 'machine', ū as in 'rule'. Unmarked a, i, u are short: a as in 'about'.
e, oAlways long: e as in 'they', o as in 'go'.
ai, auai as in 'Jai' (rhymes with 'eye'); au as in 'loud'.
n at word endAfter a long vowel (for example 'kahān', 'nahīn', 'gosāīn') the n is a nasal sound: let the vowel ring through the nose rather than saying a full n.
riThe vowel in Krishna and kripā: a light r with a short i.
shCovers both श and ष, as in 'shiv' and 'vishnu'.
ch, chhch as in 'church'; chh is the same sound with a puff of breath.
kh, gh, th, dh, ph, bhAspirated consonants: say the letter with a breath of air. 'th' is never as in 'think', and 'ph' is never 'f'.
gyज्ञ, as in gyān (knowledge), is said 'gyaa' in most of North India.
| and ||Mark the half-line and full-line pauses (। and ॥ in Devanagari).
